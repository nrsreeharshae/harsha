About

Physics as a language for building better models

I am a theoretical and computational physicist specializing in non-equilibrium statistical mechanics, plasma physics, and electron-device modeling. My research develops Generalized Langevin Equation (GLE) and fluctuation–dissipation theorem (FDT) frameworks for kinetic-to-fluid coarse-graining, non-Markovian transport, ferroelectric switching, and hardware-aware stochastic optimization.

I combine analytical theory with particle-in-cell simulation and computational benchmarking. Across these areas, the common goal is to identify the smallest dynamical description that preserves the physics needed for prediction, design, or computation.

Research

Current directions

My work connects formal statistical physics to problems in plasma transport, electron devices, and unconventional computing.

01

Kinetic-to-fluid coarse-graining

Deriving GLE–FDT closures from equilibrium fluctuations and using memory kernels to predict velocity-dependent plasma transport.

  • GLE
  • FDT
  • EPOCH PIC
02

Non-Markovian electron transport

Studying how finite memory alters space-charge-limited current, transient response, scaling laws, and terminal admittance.

  • Memory kernels
  • SCLC
  • Admittance
03

Physics-inspired optimization

Designing hardware-aware Langevin and Hamiltonian algorithms that use dissipation, noise, and rotational forces to solve hard combinatorial problems.

  • Stochastic dynamics
  • MAX-CUT
  • Hardware-aware
04

Electron-device theory

Developing analytical models for multidimensional space-charge transport and generalized Landau–Khalatnikov switching dynamics.

  • Ferroelectrics
  • Lie symmetry
  • Conformal mapping
